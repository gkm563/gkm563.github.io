01 The brief

A police fellowship, run like an operation.

APCSIP-2026 was not a workshop certificate. It was 15 days inside a working cyber crime cell — selected by exam, taught by national experts, documented like evidence.

The Amroha Police Cyber Crime Cell opened its operation to a small cohort of engineering scholars — and I had to earn the seat through a police selection pipeline first.

Selection ran like a recruitment drive: application, screening shortlist, then a 50-question cyber aptitude examination before the offer letter ever arrived. We reported at Shri Venkateshwara University, Amroha, on 10 June 2026, and the programme ran through 22 June — a valedictory, a completion certificate and a state-level award at the end.

The faculty were the reason it worked. Sessions were led by serving officers and India's top cyber security experts — Rakshit Tandon, senior consultant and advisor to law enforcement; Amit Dubey, cyber crime investigator and national security author; DSP Anjali Kataria of the Cyber Crime Cell — more than 45 officers, advisors and coordinators in total. They taught from live cases, not slides.

Mornings were briefings and law; afternoons were benches and sandboxes. Every day closed with a written log, and twelve of those logs were published on LinkedIn the same day — which is exactly how the Best Content Creator award found me.

Ask a question
What is APCSIP-2026?

The Amroha Police Cyber Security Internship Program — a 15-day law enforcement fellowship run by the Amroha Police Cyber Crime Cell under the Uttar Pradesh Police. I attended the June 2026 edition from 10 to 22 June at Shri Venkateshwara University, Amroha, covering OSINT, forensics, malware, dark web and cyber law.

Where and when did it take place?

At Shri Venkateshwara University in Amroha, Uttar Pradesh, from 10 to 22 June 2026 — reporting on the 10th, twelve days of operations from the 11th to the 22nd, and the valedictory defence and certificate on the 22nd.

Who taught the programme?

Serving officers and India's top cyber security experts — Rakshit Tandon (senior consultant and advisor to law enforcement), Amit Dubey (cyber crime investigator and national security author), DSP Anjali Kataria of the Cyber Crime Cell, DFIR specialist Yugal Pathal, dark-web researcher Saumay Srivastava and cyber law advisor Pakhi Garg — more than 45 officers and experts in total.

What award did you win?

At the valedictory on 22 June 2026, I was named Best Content Creator among all internship participants — recognition for the daily field logs documenting the programme, published live on LinkedIn. DSP Anjali Kataria presented the trophy and certificate.

How was the selection done?

An eight-gate pipeline: application in May 2026, screening shortlist on 21–22 May, a 50-question cyber aptitude examination on 24 May, the offer letter, document verification, reporting on 10 June, twelve days of operations, and the valedictory. The full chain is on this page under Clearance.

How can I verify the internship and the award?

The fellowship is logged on the roles and responsibilities ledger and in the achievements dossier, with dates and context. Twelve of the thirteen daily logs were published on LinkedIn, and the certificate and award photographs are archived in the gallery above.
