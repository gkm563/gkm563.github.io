01 The brief

A fellowship built for field work.

GIIP 2026 was not a lecture series. It was a 15-day sprint through the tools that move modern cities — with a working product due at the end.

Two weeks at the Asian Institute of Technology, learning in the same rooms and labs where its research schools build.

Selection ran through academic performance: first rank in CSE (Data Science) at United Institute of Technology and fifth college-wide across the AKTU examinations put me in the delegation sent to AIT. The programme itself was broader than a single specialism — part graduate seminar, part field school, part startup sprint.

Mornings were for frameworks: model behaviour, spatial reasoning, sensor telemetry, agentic workflows. Afternoons moved to benches, laptops and the campus green, where the same ideas had to survive an actual dataset. The teaching faculty came from AIT's research schools and from industry practice; the cohort came from several countries.

The capstone was deliberately open-ended. Our team chose a problem we had lived with in India: public transport that never gives you a reliable answer to “when is the bus?” That question became BusSetu.

01

Selected, not just enrolled

Invited as part of the United Institute of Technology delegation after finishing first in CSE (Data Science) at UIT and fifth college-wide in the AKTU examinations.

02

Labs with an artefact at the end

EpiCollect5 surveys, QGIS layers, Python EDA, Power BI dashboards, sensor rigs, robot arms and agentic workflows — each taught through something you had to make work.

03

Cross-cultural by default

A multinational cohort, faculty from AIT's research schools, one KMITL visit and a corporate-communication module on pitching across cultures.

Landed at Suvarnabhumi in the late morning and took the delegation transfer north to Pathum Thani. The first shock was scale: AIT's campus is a green research town, not a city block. Check-in at the AIT Conference Center, a quick tour, then a cohort dinner that made the next fortnight feel real.

Act 02 · Methods Days 03–06

03

Day 03 · Wed 03 Jun 2026

AI and the discipline of a good prompt

An intensive day on large-language-model behaviour: system instructions, structured output, context limits and the difference between a demo and something you can ship. Most of the value was in the constraints — getting a model to return clean JSON, then testing it against edge cases.

  • System vs user instructions, chain-of-thought and output schemas.
  • Context-window budgeting and token-management strategies.

Day 04 · Thu 04 Jun 2026

Ubiquitous GIS: the campus becomes a dataset

Field survey day. We built collection forms in EpiCollect5, walked the campus capturing spatial points, then pulled the export into QGIS. The lesson was less about software than integrity: a coordinate is only useful if you know how it was captured.

  • Mobile form design and spatial point capture with EpiCollect5.

Day 05 · Fri 05 Jun 2026

EDA: cleaning the survey before trusting it

Python, Pandas and a hundred-plus spatial points. We joined the survey export to the campus layers, checked missing values, fixed inconsistent categories and looked for the first honest patterns. Most of the day was spent on data quality — which is the point.

  • Pandas cleaning, joins and descriptive statistics on the survey set.
  • Plotting spatial distributions before making any claims.

IoT, sensors and the noise of the real world

From software to solder. The sensor benches covered microcontrollers, wiring, sampling and serial telemetry. Getting a stable reading was easy; getting a stable reading for an hour was the actual exercise. This was the day the programme's hardware thread became concrete.

  • Microcontroller setup, sensor wiring and serial output.
  • Reading noise, sampling rate and basic calibration.

Problem framing day. We mapped rider journeys, argued about scope and deliberately narrowed the first version: scheduled routes, live vehicle position, projected arrival. No payments, no accounts, no marketplace. The constraint made the next sprint possible.

A dashboard day. We built a small model, wrote DAX measures and designed for an executive reader who has ninety seconds. The recurring lesson: a chart is an argument, not decoration. Every visual had to answer a specific question.

04 Capstone

BusSetu: a live answer to waiting.

The capstone was not a slide deck. It was a working transit platform, built in the last week and defended in front of AIT faculty.

01 · Problem

Riders have schedules, not certainty

Regional bus riders work from printed timetables and habit. Vehicles disappear from view, dispatch data is fragmented and every wait becomes a guess. The cost is paid in time, missed connections and unsafe roadside waiting.

02 · Solution

Schedules in, live positions out

BusSetu ingests GTFS static schedules and blends them with GPS telemetry to project arrival times on a vector map. Riders see the route, the approaching vehicle and a live ETA without installing anything.

03 · My role

Architecture and the map layer

I led the system architecture, built the low-latency map visualiser, integrated the Google Maps APIs and presented the working prototype to the AIT panel. The rest of the team owned the surrounding flow and the demo narrative.

04 · Stack

Small, fast and deployable

Vanilla JavaScript (ES6+), Node.js, the Google Maps API, a GTFS parser, REST endpoints and Vercel Edge. The constraint was deliberate: no framework weight on the critical map view.

08 FAQ

Straight answers.

What the programme was, what it produced, and how to check the record. If something is missing, the roles ledger and achievements dossier carry the dated trail.

Ask a question
What is GIIP 2026?

GIIP 2026 is the Global Innovation Internship Program run at the Asian Institute of Technology in Thailand. The 15-day edition ran from 1 to 15 June 2026, covering AI, GIS, IoT, robotics and agentic workflows, and closed with a team capstone.

Where and when did it take place?

At the Asian Institute of Technology campus in Pathum Thani, north of central Bangkok, from 1 to 15 June 2026. Sessions used AIT teaching rooms and labs, with a KMITL visit and a cultural day in Bangkok.

What did you build during the fellowship?

BusSetu, a real-time public transport platform that combines GTFS schedules with GPS telemetry to project bus arrivals on a vector map. I led the architecture and map layer and presented the prototype to the AIT faculty panel.

Was this an internship, a course or a fellowship?

It ran as a structured international innovation programme: taught modules, lab and field work, a team capstone and a certificate at the closing ceremony. AIT's Special Degree Programs office hosted the cohort.

How can I verify the programme or the certificate?

The programme is recorded on the roles and responsibilities ledger and in the achievements dossier, with dates and context. The certificate was presented at the AIT closing ceremony, and the BusSetu prototype remains live.

What was the most useful part of the 15 days?

The forced loop from field data to a working product: collect spatial data, clean it, model it, then defend a live interface built on top of it. The BusSetu sprint turned the whole fortnight into one testable system.
