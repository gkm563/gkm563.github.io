Chapter 01 · The origin story

From zero to upstream.

Before the awards in Gurugram and the +2 reviews on Gerrit, there was a hostel room, a terminal full of red Jenkins builds, and a student learning in public.

01 Rejection as fuel 02 The Gerrit maze 03 Mentors on Meet 04 Shipping velocity
012025 → Mar 2026

Turning rejection into rocket fuel

In 2025 I applied as a Community Volunteer for GDG Prayagraj and was rejected. It stung — but the decision was simple: treat rejection as feedback, not a verdict. I kept sharpening my programming fundamentals, showing up to meetups, and contributing wherever I could.

In March 2026 that persistence paid off. I was selected as a Community Volunteer for GDG Prayagraj 2026–27, mentored by Ankit Sir and Mohd Shadab Sir. The compounding-effort mindset that got me there is the same one that carried me through Road to Wiki.

Lesson — consistency outperforms talent, every single time.

02Jan – Feb 2026

The midnight tutorials and the Gerrit maze

Opening the MediaWiki repository for the first time was intimidating: millions of lines, unfamiliar PHP structures, and Gerrit — an infrastructure with no friendly green merge button. Nothing about it looked like a GitHub pull request.

At 2 AM I watched YouTube videos on SSH keys, commit hooks, Change-Id footers and rebasing. My first patchset failed CI again and again. Every red build forced me to read the stack trace properly, understand the git history, and push again until Jenkins went green.

Lesson — you don't master Git in class; you master it in merge conflicts.

03Feb – May 2026

Google Meet calls and the power of a community

The weekly Google Meet sessions became my sanctuary. Mentors — Sanskar Dubey, Sarthak Parashar, Aditya and Pratik Singh (GitLab) — broke intimidating concepts into steps you could actually execute: Phabricator workboards, clean commit messages, accepting review feedback with grace.

When Ankit Sir publicly acknowledged my commitment during the RTW-P2S3 session with Wikimedia Principal Engineer Santhosh Thottingal, something clicked: I belong here.

Lesson — great mentors don't write your code; they give you the courage to ship it.

04Apr – Oct 2026

From the first merge to 30+ production patches

Nothing compares to the moment a core Wikimedia engineer marks Code-Review +2 and Gerrit merges your work into production. The first merge broke the psychological barrier — contributing to global software stopped being magic and became structured problem-solving.

I authored patches through college exams, took 1st rank in the one-week contribution marathon, landed 8 featured patches in the official OKI-IIIT Impact Report, engineered Proofread & translatewiki tooling, and crossed 30+ merged patches. The student watching YouTube tutorials had become an upstream builder.

Outcome — 30+ merges, three awards, and a campus mandate to teach others.

“

Nobody starts knowing how Gerrit works or how MediaWiki routes requests. We all start at zero. The difference is whether you close the terminal when Jenkins throws an error — or search until you find the solution.

Chapter 10 · Questions

Straight answers.

The five things people ask most about Road to Wiki Cohort 2 — and where to check each one yourself.

Open the sources
What is Road to Wiki Cohort 2?

Road to Wiki is the flagship technical program of WikiClub Tech India, run with the Open Knowledge Initiatives (OKI) at IIIT Hyderabad and supported by the Wikimedia Foundation. Cohort 2 brought 90+ students from 12 partner universities through three phases across 8–9 months — from January to the October 2026 graduation in Gurugram.

How many upstream patches did you merge?

30+ across Wikimedia repositories. Eight were featured in the WikiClub Tech Technical Impact Report I (January–June 2026), spanning Pywikibot, the Function Orchestrator, MinervaNeue, GrowthExperiments, MediaWiki core, UploadWizard and ProofreadPage. The patch ledger links each one.

Which awards did you win at graduation?

Three: Best Contributor, Most Active Contributor, and 1st Rank in the one-week WikiClub Tech marathon. All three were presented at the conclave in Gurugram on 2–4 October 2026.

How can I verify the contributions independently?

Every patch sits on the Wikimedia Gerrit under owner:gkm563, tasks appear on Phabricator as Gkm563, and the program record lives on Meta under IIITH-OKI/Wikiclub Tech/Road To Wiki, including the Contribution Ledger and The Celebration Day pages.

What does “a community is a loop, not a line” mean?

Learners enter, grow into contributors, and the strongest return as mentors for the next cohort. I crossed that loop during Cohort 2 and now mentor juniors at United Institute of Technology, Prayagraj, as a WikiClub Tech campus envoy.
